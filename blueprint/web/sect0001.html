Definition 1.1 Analytic Degree and Mitsuishi Comparison Bridge
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • AlexandrovConsequences.analyticDegree
  • AlexandrovConsequences.MitsuishiDegreeBridge

Let \((X, \mathsf{d}, \mu _X)\) be a finite-measure space and \(Y\) a closed oriented Riemannian \(N\)-manifold with volume \(\operatorname {Vol}(Y) {\gt} 0\). The analytic degree of a map \(f : X \to Y\) with signed metric Jacobian \(\mathrm{sJac}_f\) is

\[ \deg _{\mathrm{an}}(f) := \frac{1}{\operatorname {Vol}(Y)} \int _X \mathrm{sJac}_f(x)\, d\mu _X(x). \]

A Mitsuishi degree bridge packages \(Y\) together with a fundamental current, singular homology degree \(\deg _{\mathrm{top}}(f)\), current pushforward degree \(\deg _{\mathrm{curr}}(f)\), and the naturality identity \(\deg _{\mathrm{curr}}(f) = \deg _{\mathrm{top}}(f)\) induced by Mitsuishi’s comparison isomorphism \(\Psi : H_N^{\mathbf{I}}(X) \xrightarrow {\sim } H_N(X;\mathbb {Z})\).

Lemma 1.2 Analytic Degree Integral Bound

For any integrable signed metric Jacobian \(\mathrm{sJac}_f\) and \(\operatorname {Vol}(Y) {\gt} 0\), \(|\deg _{\mathrm{an}}(f)|\, \operatorname {Vol}(Y) \le \int _X |\mathrm{sJac}_f(x)|\, d\mu _X(x)\).

Proof ▶

Cancel \(\operatorname {Vol}(Y)^{-1} \cdot \operatorname {Vol}(Y) = 1\) using \(\operatorname {Vol}(Y) {\gt} 0\) and apply the triangle inequality for the Bochner integral.

Theorem 1.3 Analytic Degree Equals Topological Degree on Class \(\mathcal{S}\) Spaces

On any class \(\mathcal{S}\) space \(X\) (compact orientable \(N\)-metric space with \(\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(\mathcal{S}_X) = 0\) and Mitsuishi comparison isomorphism \(\Psi \)), every Lipschitz map \(f : X \to Y\) satisfies Kirchheim’s signed area formula and hence

\[ \deg _{\mathrm{an}}(f) = \deg _{\mathrm{top}}(f). \]
Proof ▶

Substituting Kirchheim’s signed area identity \(\int _X \mathrm{sJac}_f\, d\mathcal{H}^N = \deg _{\mathrm{curr}}(f)\, \mathcal{H}^N(Y)\) into \(\deg _{\mathrm{an}}(f)\) cancels \(\mathcal{H}^N(Y)\), and Mitsuishi naturality yields \(\deg _{\mathrm{top}}(f)\).

Theorem 1.4 Theorem 1.1: Alexandrov Entropy-Degree Theorem and Smoothness Rigidity
✓

Let \(X\) be a closed orientable \(N\)-dimensional Alexandrov space without boundary (\(\mathrm{curv} \ge \kappa \)) and \(f : X \to Y\) a continuous map into a closed orientable negatively curved locally symmetric \(N\)-manifold \(Y\). Via Lipschitz approximation \(f_{\mathrm{lip}} \simeq f\),

\[ |\deg (f)| \, h(\widetilde{Y})^N \, \mathcal{H}^N(Y) \le h(\overline{X})^N \, \mathcal{H}^N(X). \]

Furthermore, if equality holds together with \(|\deg (f)| = [\pi _1(Y) : f_*\pi _1(X)] \ge 1\), then the rescaled limit map \(\hat{F}_\infty : (X, c\, \mathsf{d}_X) \to \widehat{Y}\) (\(c = h(\overline{X})/h(\widetilde{Y}) {\gt} 0\)) is a surjective global homothety onto the degree-\(|\deg (f)|\) cover \(\widehat{Y}\), and hence \(\mathcal{S}(X) = \emptyset \).

Proof ▶

Homotopy invariance equates \(\deg (f) = \deg (f_{\mathrm{lip}})\) in RCDEntropyDegree.RCD.alexandrov_entropy_degree_inequality, and equality rigidity transfers smoothness from \(\widehat{Y}\) via RCDEntropyDegree.RCD.rcd_entropy_degree_equality_rigidity.

Lemma 1.5 Remark 1.15: Necessity of the Index Condition and Connected-Sum Counterexample

In the covering factorization \(|\deg (f)| = |\deg (\hat{f})| \cdot [\pi _1(Y) : f_*\pi _1(X)]\) with \(\deg (f) \neq 0\), we have \(|\deg (f)| = [\pi _1(Y) : f_*\pi _1(X)] \iff |\deg (\hat{f})| = 1\). For the pinch map \(Y \# Y \to Y \vee Y \to Y\) (\(b_1(Y) \ge 1\)), \(|\deg (f)| = 2 {\gt} 1 = [\pi _1(Y):f_*\pi _1(X)]\) and \(b_1(Y \# Y) = 2 b_1(Y) \neq b_1(Y)\).

Proof ▶

Cancel the positive integer index \([\pi _1(Y) : f_*\pi _1(X)] \ge 1\) in the degree factorization, and compare \(2 b_1(Y) \neq b_1(Y)\) for \(b_1(Y) \ge 1\).

Lemma 1.6 Strictness, Entropy Bounds, and Metric Doubling Cancellation

  1. Whenever \(\mathcal{S}_X \neq \emptyset \), \(|\deg (f)| = [\pi _1(Y):f_*\pi _1(X)] \ge 1\), and equality forces \(\mathcal{S}_X = \emptyset \), the entropy-volume inequality is strict (\({\lt}\)).

  2. Whenever \(0 \le h(\overline{X}) \le h_0\) (\(h_0 {\gt} 0\)) and \(h(\widetilde{Y}) = h_0\), Theorem 1.1 yields \(|\deg (f)|\operatorname {Vol}(Y) \le \mathcal{H}^N(X)\) (and \({\lt}\) when strict).

  3. Under Perelman/Petrunin doubling \(\mathcal{H}^N(DZ) = 2\mathcal{H}^N(Z)\) and \(\mathcal{H}^N(DY) = 2\mathcal{H}^N(Y)\), cancelling \(2 h_0^N {\gt} 0\) gives \(|k|\mathcal{H}^N(Y) \le \mathcal{H}^N(Z)\).

Proof ▶

Any equality contradicts \(\mathcal{S}_X \neq \emptyset \); monotonicity of \(x \mapsto x^N\) on \([0,\infty )\) and positivity of \(h_0^N\) and \(2 h_0^N\) give the volume and doubling bounds.
